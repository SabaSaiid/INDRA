## 📌 1. Official Problem Statement Description

> **"Design and develop a scalable National Weather Big Data Analytics Platform capable of collecting and processing real-time weather-related information for India from multiple internet-based sources including social media platforms, public datasets, websites, APIs, and citizen reports.**
>
> **The platform should automatically collect weather related posts and information tagged with #IMD and other relevant weather hashtags, along with metadata such as date & time, city, state, GPS location, photos, videos, and event category, and store the information in a centralized database.**
>
> **The system should leverage big data technologies and open-source tools to support large-scale real-time data ingestion, processing, storage, and visualization.**
>
> **Participants are encouraged to use machine learning and AI-based techniques to identify fake or misleading reports, verify untrusted sources, remove duplicate entries, and automatically categorize weather events such as rainfall, thunderstorms, flooding, heatwaves, fog, dust storms, and strong winds.**
>
> **Develop a web-based dashboard and Admin Panel for monitoring and analysing collected data with features including:**
> - **Date-wise filtering**
> - **Event-wise filtering**
> - **Location-wise filtering**
> - **Verification status tracking**
> - **Real-time visualization and analytics"**

## ⚖️ 2. Core Philosophy: Why INDRA is Built This Way

Standard weather apps merely answer: *"What is the weather in city X?"* (1 API call $\rightarrow$ 1 UI display).  
**INDRA** answers: **"What real weather events are unfolding, how severe are they, how reliable is the data, and what is the exact verifiable evidence?"**

In a severe crisis (flash floods, cloudbursts, cyclones), disaster management authorities suffer from **report fragmentation and alert fatigue**. INDRA fuses scattered, unverified reports into **one verified event** with an unalterable, explainable **Verification Receipt** and a **SHA-256 hash-chained audit trail**.

## 🔍 4. Deep-Dive: What We Have Done (Detailed Breakdown)

### 1. Ingestion & Streaming Highway (Tier 1 & Tier 2)
* **Real-Time Citizen Ingestion:** Public REST endpoint accepts citizen reports with validation. Rejects coordinates outside India's geographic polygon with HTTP `422` rather than snapping them to the map.
* **Redpanda/Kafka Decoupling:** Ingested reports write immediately to `indra.raw.reports`. A consumer service processes them asynchronously so sudden surges (e.g. 10,000 reports during a cyclone) never drop requests.
* **Live Weather Poller:** Every 10 minutes, [`station_poller.py`](file:///Users/sabasaeed/0_Saba%20CSE/Hackathons/SIH%2026/INDRA/backend/app/workers/station_poller.py) fetches 24-hour accumulated rainfall for Indian cities from Open-Meteo into `station_readings`. The scoring engine checks this table before calling the external API.
* **Official Alerts Poller:** Every 5 minutes, [`sachet_poller.py`](file:///Users/sabasaeed/0_Saba%20CSE/Hackathons/SIH%2026/INDRA/backend/app/workers/sachet_poller.py) parses NDMA's SACHET CAP XML feed into `agency_alerts`.

### 2. Processing & Deduplication Engine (Tier 2 & Tier 3)
* **Sentence-Transformers Deduplication:** When a report arrives, its text is embedded using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`. If cosine similarity $\ge 0.88$, spatial distance $\le 1\text{ km}$, and time difference $\le 15\text{ minutes}$, it is recorded as a `duplicate_of` the original report.
* **No False Inflation:** Suppressed duplicates are excluded from spatial clustering. A viral tweet or repeated text sent 50 times will **never inflate event severity or corroborate an event**.
* **Text Mining & Water Depth Extraction:** Regular expressions and gazetteer dictionaries in [`services/text_processing.py`](file:///Users/sabasaeed/0_Saba%20CSE/Hackathons/SIH%2026/INDRA/backend/app/services/text_processing.py) extract depth phrases (e.g., *"knee deep"* $\rightarrow$ 50 cm, *"waist deep"* $\rightarrow$ 100 cm) and operational hazard keywords.

### 3. Geospatial Analytics & Fusion (Tier 3)
* **Spatial DBSCAN Clustering:** Reports are clustered using Scikit-Learn’s DBSCAN with a **great-circle haversine metric** (5 km physical radius).
* **Uber H3 Indexing:** Reports are assigned H3 resolution-8 spatial cells. Heatmaps aggregate at resolutions 6, 7, and 8 via `GET /api/geo/heatmap`.
* **Event Boundary Polygons:** Every formed event calculates a convex or 250 m-buffered concave hull containing all constituent non-duplicate reports, served as GeoJSON to the dashboard.
* **Corroboration Threshold:** A single isolated citizen report cannot form an event. DBSCAN requires at least 2 reports.

### 4. The Verification Receipt & Evidence Fusion (Tier 3 & Tier 4)
* **100-Point Deterministic Formula:**
  $$\text{Confidence} = \frac{\sum_{\text{online}} (\text{Weight} \times \text{Score})}{\sum_{\text{online}} \text{Weight}}$$
  Factors include:
  1. *Weather Agreement (25%)*: Cross-referenced against IMD rainfall thresholds (2.5mm light, 15.6mm moderate, 64.5mm heavy).
  2. *Independent Reports (20%)*: Logarithmic density scaling of independent witnesses.
  3. *Spatio-Temporal Proximity (20%)*: Cluster compactness in meters.
  4. *Source Reliability (15%)*: Prior credibility based on submitting source.
  5. *Vision Analysis (15%)*: *Permanently offline (reason published).*
  6. *Historical Anomaly (5%)*: *Permanently offline (reason published).*
* **Honest Factor Coverage:** If vision and anomaly detection are offline, `factor_coverage = 0.80`. The confidence score is normalised over the remaining 80%, and the coverage is prominently displayed alongside the score.
* **Severity Driven by Reality:** Severity is derived from measured depth ($\ge 120\text{ cm} \rightarrow \text{CRITICAL}$, $\ge 60\text{ cm} \rightarrow \text{HIGH}$, $\ge 20\text{ cm} \rightarrow \text{MODERATE}$) or witness corroboration count, not an opaque model.

### 5. Web Dashboard & Command Center (Tier 4)
* **Live Tactical Map:** Leaflet map fitting all of India, showing real-time event polygons, weather station readings, and active government warnings.
* **Multi-Dimensional Filters:** Full filtering by date range (24H, 7D, 30D, ALL), hazard event types, severity levels, and geographic districts.
* **Verification Receipt Modal:** Command Center operators can inspect the mathematical evidence behind any event.
* **Human-in-the-Loop Review Queue:** Events between 60% and 90% confidence (or high-severity claims) are routed to `PENDING_HUMAN_REVIEW` or `QUARANTINED`, where a Commander can approve, reject, or override severity via `PATCH /api/events/{id}/review`.
* **Recent 24-Sep Frontend Repairs:** 
  - Recent events and event distribution donut now show live official SACHET warnings when 0 active events exist.
  - Live feed displays unified reports, events, and warnings formatted in Indian Standard Time (IST).
  - Tactical map fills full screen dynamically.

### 6. Security & Auditability
* **Tamper-Evident SHA-256 Hash Chain:** Every automated pipeline action (`AUTO_VERIFY`, `QUARANTINE`) and every commander action (`HUMAN_APPROVE`, `MANUAL_OVERRIDE`) appends a record with `prev_hash` linked cryptographically.
* **PostgreSQL Trigger Protection:** A database trigger blocks `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on `audit_logs`.
* **948 Automated Backend Tests:** Full test suite verifying auth gates, determinism, clustering, and data contracts.

## 🚫 5. What We Have NOT Done (And Why)

Every item not built was a deliberate, defensible architectural choice rather than an oversight:

### 1. Social Media Scraper (`#IMD` Twitter/X Feed)
* **Why it's not built:** Access to Twitter/X’s streaming API now requires costly enterprise credentials. Free-tier scrapers break unpredictably during live presentations.
* **What we do instead:** The database schema and source type matrix natively support `TWITTER_IMD` with a 0.50 reliability prior, ready for live webhook streaming once credentials are provided.

### 2. Machine Learning Event Classification Model
* **Why it's not built:** An NLP model was trained on 300 domain-specific records (`data/labelled/reports_v1.csv`). Testing revealed a macro-F1 of **0.787**, with a `NOT_RELEVANT` recall of 0.667 and **5 out of 72 severe flood reports dismissed**.
* **The Defense:** A model that discards 1 in 14 real floods is dangerous for disaster management. Rather than deploy an unsafe model, `classify()` returns `None`, and the platform relies on citizen hazard tags and regex extraction.

### 3. Computer Vision & Flood Image Verification
* **Why it's not built:** Reliable flood depth estimation from arbitrary camera angles requires calibrated multi-camera telemetry or LiDAR.
* **The Defense:** Instead of returning a fake random image score (which early prototypes did), INDRA declares `vision_analysis: offline` on every receipt and drops `factor_coverage` to `0.80`. Stating that a signal is absent is strictly better than faking it.

### 4. Direct IMD Radar / CWC Gauge Telemetry
* **Why it's not built:** Government Doppler radar and river gauge APIs require official MoES ministry credentials and VPN access.
* **What we do instead:** We integrated Open-Meteo's high-resolution precipitation API as a real-time surrogate and NDMA's SACHET for official warnings.

### 5. Automated Citizen SMS / CAP Alert Broadcasting
* **Why it's not built:** An unverified hackathon system blasting automated SMS alerts to the public creates panic.
* **The Defense:** In disaster management, automated dispatch based on a 0.50 confidence score is dangerous. INDRA routes verified intelligence to the **Command Center**, where human nodal officers authorize public broadcasts.

### Feature 8: C-DOT CAP v1.2 Protocol Compliance & XML Schema Mapping

* **Objective:** Full interoperability with India's National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) and C-DOT National Alert System via standard ITU-T X.1303 / OASIS Common Alerting Protocol (CAP v1.2).
* **Technical Specification & XML Serialization:**
  1. **CAP `<alert>` Header Mapping:**
     * `identifier`: Cryptographic SHA-256 hash formatted as `INDRA-ALERT-YYYYMMDD-{uuid}`.
     * `sender`: `indra.seoc.ndma.gov.in` (authenticated officer callsign).
     * `status`: `Actual` (for production) or `Exercise` / `Test` (for simulation).
     * `msgType`: `Alert`, `Update`, or `Cancel`.
     * `scope`: `Public` or `Restricted`.
  2. **CAP `<info>` Segment Attributes:**
     * `category`: `Met` (Meteorological) or `Geo` (Geophysical / Landslide).
     * `event`: Hazard category (`Urban Flood`, `Flash Flood`, `Cyclone`, `Cloudburst`).
     * `urgency`: `Immediate` ($< 1\text{ hour}$ response required) or `Expected` ($< 6\text{ hours}$).
     * `severity`: `Extreme` (threat to life/property), `Severe`, or `Moderate`.
     * `certainty`: `Observed` (confirmed by physical stations + crowd consensus) or `Likely` ($p > 0.70$).
  3. **Geospatial `<area>` Geometry:**
     * Translates PostGIS concave hull boundaries into standard WGS84 `<polygon>` coordinate strings (`lat,lon lat,lon ...`).
     * Attaches Census 2011 standard LGD (Local Government Directory) district codes under `<geocode>` for automated Cell Broadcast Service (CBS) routing by telecom service providers.
